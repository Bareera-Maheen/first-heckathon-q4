# Glossary

## Learning Objectives
- Familiarize yourself with key terminology and acronyms used in Physical AI and Humanoid Robotics.
- Enhance comprehension of complex concepts through concise definitions.
- Serve as a quick reference for technical terms encountered throughout the textbook.

<h2>Core Concepts</h2>
This glossary provides definitions for important terms, acronyms, and concepts frequently used in the domains of Physical AI and Humanoid Robotics. Understanding this specialized vocabulary is essential for effective learning and communication within the field.

### A
- **Actuator**: A component that converts energy into motion, enabling a robot to perform physical actions. (e.g., motors, hydraulic cylinders).
- **AI (Artificial Intelligence)**: The simulation of human intelligence processes by machines, especially computer systems. These processes include learning, reasoning, and self-correction.
- **Autonomous System**: A system capable of operating independently without human intervention, often relying on sensors, AI, and control systems.

### B
- **Bipedal Robot**: A robot that moves on two legs, mimicking human locomotion.
- **BLDC (Brushless DC) Motor**: A highly efficient electric motor often used in robotics for its power-to-weight ratio and precise control.

### C
- **Centroidal Dynamics**: A simplified dynamic model focusing on the motion of a robot's center of mass and angular momentum, particularly useful for balance control in legged robots.
- **Closed-Loop Control**: A control system that uses feedback from sensors to continuously adjust its output and minimize error.
- **CoM (Center of Mass)**: The unique point where the weighted relative position of the distributed mass sums to zero. Crucial for robot stability.
- **Control System**: The mechanism that manages, commands, directs, or regulates the behavior of other devices or systems.

### D
- **D-H Parameters (Denavit-Hartenberg Parameters)**: A standard convention used to model the kinematic chains of robotic manipulators.
- **Dynamics**: The study of motion considering the forces and torques that cause it.
- **DOF (Degrees of Freedom)**: The number of independent parameters that define the configuration of a mechanical system (e.g., number of joints in a robot arm).

### E
- **EAP (Electroactive Polymer)**: A polymer that exhibits a change in size or shape when stimulated by an electric field, often referred to as "artificial muscles."
- **Encoder**: A sensor that converts angular or linear position into an analog or digital signal.
- **End-Effector**: The device or tool located at the end of a robotic arm, designed to interact with the environment (e.g., gripper, welding torch).

### F
- **Forward Kinematics**: The process of calculating the position and orientation of the end-effector given the joint variables of a robot.
- **Forward Dynamics**: Calculating the resulting accelerations of a robot's joints given the applied forces and torques.

### H
- **Haptic Sensor**: A sensor that provides information related to touch and force interaction.
- **Humanoid Robot**: A robot with its body shape built to resemble the human body.

### I
- **IK (Inverse Kinematics)**: The process of calculating the joint variables required to achieve a desired position and orientation of the end-effector.
- **IMU (Inertial Measurement Unit)**: An electronic device that measures and reports a body's specific force, angular rate, and sometimes the magnetic field surrounding the body, using a combination of accelerometers and gyroscopes.
- **Inverse Dynamics**: Calculating the forces and torques required at each joint to achieve a desired motion.

### K
- **Kinematics**: The study of motion without considering the forces that cause it.

### L
- **Learning from Demonstration (LfD)**: A machine learning paradigm where a robot learns new skills by observing human examples.
- **LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging)**: A remote sensing method that uses light in the form of a pulsed laser to measure ranges (variable distances) to the Earth.

### M
- **MPC (Model Predictive Control)**: An advanced control method that uses a system model to predict future behavior and optimize control inputs over a receding horizon.

### N
- **Node (ROS 2)**: An executable process in the ROS 2 computational graph.

### P
- **Perception**: The process by which robots acquire, interpret, and understand information from their environment using sensors.
- **PID Controller (Proportional-Integral-Derivative Controller)**: A common feedback control loop mechanism widely used in industrial control systems.
- **Proprioceptive Sensor**: A sensor that measures the internal state of the robot, such as joint angles or motor speeds.
- **pytest**: A popular Python testing framework used for writing concise and scalable tests.

### R
- **`rclpy`**: The Python client library for ROS 2, enabling Python programs to interact with the ROS 2 graph.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: A machine learning approach where an agent learns to make decisions by performing actions in an environment to maximize a cumulative reward.
- **ROS 2 (Robot Operating System 2)**: A flexible framework for writing robot software, providing tools, libraries, and conventions.

### S
- **SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping)**: A computational problem of constructing or updating a map of an unknown environment while simultaneously keeping track of an agent's location within it.
- **SMA (Shape Memory Alloy)**: An alloy that can be deformed at one temperature and then return to its original shape upon heating.
- **Sensor**: A device that detects or measures a physical property and records, indicates, or otherwise responds to it.
- **Service (ROS 2)**: A request/reply communication mechanism in ROS 2.
- **Sidebar**: A navigation menu typically found on the side of a webpage, listing content sections.
- **Support Polygon**: The convex hull of a robot's ground contact points; used in ZMP criterion for stability.

### T
- **Tactile Sensor**: A sensor providing a sense of touch, detecting pressure, force, or texture.
- **Topic (ROS 2)**: An anonymous publish/subscribe message bus in ROS 2.

### U
- **URDF (Unified Robot Description Format)**: An XML file format used in ROS to describe all elements of a robot.

### Z
- **ZMP (Zero Moment Point)**: A criterion for dynamic stability in legged robots, indicating the point on the ground where the total moment of forces is zero.
